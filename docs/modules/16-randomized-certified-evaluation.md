# Module 16: Randomized Certified Evaluation

## Status

Dependent, planned. Research and requirements only; generation, shortcut resistance, split integrity and deep feasibility are not yet established. Creating this module authorizes no implementation or model calls by itself.

## Type

Generator foundation, mathematical feasibility and evaluation integrity.

## Purpose

Generate varied, reproducible function/domain tasks whose labels are trustworthy and whose difficulty cannot be explained by the specific shortcuts we have tested. Randomize meaningful mathematical structure rather than only constants in a fixed template. Distinguish randomness, certification, diversity and observed difficulty as separate properties.

The user wants demanding tasks that cannot be solved through a superficial rule. The operational target is resistance to a declared set of superficial heuristics, not a guarantee that no simple valid mathematical solution exists. Correct simplification and sound composition rules remain legitimate solution methods. High model scores are valid outcomes.

The [research record](../research/randomized-evaluation-design-v1.md) distinguishes source paraphrases, project-specific design proposals and unresolved hypotheses. Its sources motivate this module; they do not prove that a proposed generator is difficult or free from shortcuts.

## Finished Outcome

A separately versioned random generator and admission policy produce frozen certified tasks, complete candidate/rejection records, meaningful related-task clusters, independently checked reference evidence and reproducible quality reports. A consuming study can identify exactly which accepted distribution it measures and which shortcuts and split conditions were checked.

A bounded supported implementation may receive a scoped Go while deeper targets remain unsupported. An honest No-go is the required result when mathematical validity, requested coverage or split integrity cannot be established. A scoped implementation must not claim that Module 15's 512-stage target is delivered.

## Why It Comes Here

The repaired `composition-v1` contains real nonlinear stages but only two increasing convex atoms, three sequence patterns and nine base-chain groups. It still permits coefficient, contraction and familiar-structure cues. Earlier associative sums demonstrate that large tree depth can overstate dependent composition. These are documented boundaries, not invalid historical grades.

Module 15 owns the fixed deep study and its execution. Module 16 owns the reusable randomized construction, accepted-population definition, structural split checks and shortcut diagnostics. Module 16 can proceed offline alongside Module 15's deep-reference feasibility; neither depends on completion of the other's model experiment. Mini module 14a remains the immediate product task before Module 15 implementation or new study calls. Their outputs meet before Module 15 freezes a population described as randomized and checked for shortcuts.

## Detailed Work

### 1. Define the object being sampled

A task is the exact rendered scalar function on its exact finite, nondegenerate closed interval. The primary property is convex-only, concave-only or neither on that interval; affine/constant overlap is excluded. The first implementation should stay within an explicitly supported smooth subset unless a separately proved nonsmooth policy is supplied.

The sampling specification must name the grammar, operator probabilities, stopping/size rules, chain/branch proportions, exact coefficient grid or distribution, parameter dependencies, domain policy, label/level/mechanism quotas and certifier-selection policy. A distribution over trees is not automatically uniform over sizes, functions or difficulty. Do not advertise uniform randomness over all mathematical functions.

Track separately: proposal distribution; mathematically valid proposals; certified accepted proposals; quota-balanced population; and any additional challenge selection. Do not use inverse-probability correction unless actual inclusion probabilities and assumptions are justified. Otherwise report conditional scores for the named accepted population.

### 2. Randomize structure with proof obligations

Support randomized chain lengths and branching shapes within declared bounds. Choose variable-dependent subexpressions, signed rational parameters, valid intervals, and the locations of nonlinear/domain-sensitive operations. Generation must not always build one known convex core and subtract a class-specific correction.

Evaluate three proposed routes during feasibility:

| Route | Construction behavior | Required evidence and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Property-directed construction | Carry domain, range, sign, monotonicity and curvature obligations while sampling compatible operations and branches. | Prove the composition steps and exclude opposite-label overlap. DCP recognition and shortcut coverage remain separate diagnostics; valid easy cases must remain in the ordinary sample. |
| Proposal then certify | Sample from a bounded grammar without assuming the desired output label, then determine admissible labels from independent proof. | Retain invalid, affine, unresolved and rejected proposals. A requested quota never overrides a certificate or turns unknown into neither. |
| Exact curvature construction | Sample an exact sign-certified curvature expression, construct a function with that second derivative, and independently verify the actual rendered expression identity. | Record construction ancestry and parameter cues. Random coefficients in one integrated template are not broad-family coverage; depth is measured after rendering, not inferred from polynomial degree. |

These are workstreams to prove, not implemented capabilities. Freeze the achieved route set and weights before final generation; unsupported routes receive explicit No-go results. Do not claim that every root operation or proof route can realize every label. Publish impossible or unachieved coverage cells and check label cues in the achieved support.

For each approved atom, document exact semantics, domain, admissible input ranges, derivative/curvature rules, and boundary behavior. Existing operations are the starting scope; exponential, logarithmic and square-root combinations require reliable range/domain propagation. Competing variable-dependent branches and interior transitions must have defensible parameter regions, not merely visually complicated formulas.

### 3. Establish reference validity independently

For a twice continuously differentiable function on a neighborhood of the closed interval, accepted reference evidence must support one of:

| Label | Required mathematical evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Convex-only | Nonnegative second derivative throughout the interval, plus a strict-positive interior second-derivative witness or another valid exclusion of concavity. |
| Concave-only | Nonpositive second derivative throughout the interval, plus a strict-negative interior second-derivative witness or another valid exclusion of convexity. |
| Neither | Both strict-positive and strict-negative interior second-derivative witnesses, with full domain/regularity validity; or rigorously checked strict Jensen violations in both directions under the relevant reference policy. |

An everywhere-positive curvature margin is sufficient but not necessary. Do not reject every zero-curvature point as mathematically invalid; distinguish a deliberately restricted margin policy from mathematical truth. Keep real-valuedness, positive log arguments, nonzero denominators and required differentiability throughout the domain explicit.

Bind proofs to the actual AST, rendering and interval sent to the model. Independently check derivatives, rational identities, range claims and strict witnesses using a separately justified implementation or analytic argument. Calling the generator's label routine again is replay, not independence. Parser/renderer round trips must preserve semantics; this check must not introduce an unreviewed expression evaluator.

DCP failure, interval overlap, numerical sampling, a failed proof search or a resource limit never proves neither. Reference disagreement blocks admission. Record software, arithmetic assumptions, precision/work policies, reviewer roles and uncertainty honestly; AI-assisted review is not human expert or proof-assistant approval.

### 4. Make randomness reproducible and operationally bounded

Freeze the random algorithm/version, seed encoding, probability mapping and test vectors. Separate random streams for structure, parameters, domains, candidate ordering, splits and diagnostic controls. Assign stable candidate ordinals so parallel completion order cannot determine membership. Persist actual candidates, accepted expressions, domains and certificates; a seed alone is insufficient reproduction evidence.

For a fixed specification and environment, serial and parallel generation must yield the same ordered proposals; identical retained proof outcomes under deterministic admission must yield identical accepted membership. If wall-clock certification cutoffs produce different proof outcomes, record the discrepancy and block a reproducible freeze until resolved or covered by a separately declared stable admission policy. Use the first qualifying candidates in the frozen ordinal policy, not whichever certificates return fastest. Runtime/resource uncertainty stays in the ledger; replay must not silently replace a timed-out member with another draw.

Specify finite attempts per coverage cell, total proposals, retained bytes, proof work, arithmetic growth and process deadlines before production generation. Exhaustion yields incomplete coverage with reasons and no completed-suite identity. Lowering the target, switching atoms or widening a proof boundary requires a versioned pre-run amendment.

Reuse current native limits where applicable: operation depth 32, 256 node occurrences, bounded batches and bounded certification work. Module 15 owns separate deep-family proof policies where needed. Neither module may change the standard numerical policy merely to reach 512 stages.

Record proposed, domain-invalid, unsupported, affine/overlap, duplicate, unresolved, resource-exhausted, certified, quota-excluded, diagnostic-excluded and accepted counts by route/level/mechanism/label regime. Define exclusive terminal candidate outcomes, with secondary diagnostic flags kept separate. Include elapsed work and why admission failed. This is a verifier-selected distribution; high acceptance is not proof of correctness and low acceptance is not model difficulty.

### 5. Detect manufactured depth and related tasks

Retain raw AST depth, longest nonlinear variable-dependent path, node occurrences, unique subtrees, branch count, operator histogram, rendered size, bounded-simplified structure, range/derivative scales and certified margins. A nonlinear stage must consume a genuinely variable-dependent result. Reassociation, affine wrappers and inactive branches must not supply claimed nonlinear stages.

Audit exact duplicates, associative flattening, constant/affine reductions, double negation, zero/one arithmetic, inverse pairs under their actual domains, known repeated-atom closed forms and constant branches. Simplifications require domain-preserving identities; for example, sqrt(u²) equals |u|, not u without a sign proof. Keep original model inputs and raw measurements unchanged.

Related-task groups must carry parent construction, significant shared variable-dependent cores/prefixes, parameter groups, domain variants, transformations, counterfactual pairs and reduced examples. Shared primitive atoms alone do not make every task one cluster. Define the meaningful-core rule and transitive grouping policy in D-15, including conflicts between family tags and shared ancestry.

Exact distinctness, a new seed, a different coefficient, or a different AST hash does not establish independent support. Bounded normalization cannot prove arbitrary semantic nonequivalence. Report unknown equivalence and residual split risk rather than inventing certainty.

### 6. Test declared shortcuts without excluding valid mathematics

Run cheap, frozen baselines on development/validation partitions before final evaluation. Train any statistical predictor only on its permitted development data and group splits; labels from final evaluation cannot tune baselines, thresholds or filters.

| Baseline or diagnostic | What it tests | Required interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constant/majority and label-prior predictions | Label imbalance and metric behavior. | Report accuracy and macro-F1 separately; on exactly balanced three-class data, always predicting one class gives accuracy 1/3 and macro-F1 1/6. Use actual counts for approximately balanced levels. |
| Root operation, outer sign and sign parity | Class cues from visible outer form. | Test both examples that support a heuristic and certified counterexamples to it. Never select only the cases it misclassifies. |
| Coefficient/domain/length/operator summaries | Distributional cues without full dependent reasoning. | Use matched strata and group-held-out evaluation. A successful shallow classifier is a generator warning, not proof of a model's internal reasoning. |
| Shallow subtree/truncated-structure features | Whether most of the expression can be ignored. | Retain budget/support and missing-feature behavior; do not silently present a truncated expression as the original problem. |
| Predetermined sparse derivative/Jensen probes | Reliance on finite sampling as if it were a global certificate. | Treat predictions as heuristic. Include strict interior-transition controls and easy valid cases, with proved references. |
| Bounded simplification plus sound proof rules | Whether a legitimate concise mathematical argument solves the task. | Report solved and abstained coverage. Correct sound shortcuts are valid solutions; unknown/abstention is not a wrong mathematical answer. |

Before looking at final results, D-16 must define baseline features, training/search budget, comparison population, uncertainty method, effect tolerance and a multiple-baseline decision rule. Compare against label priors and cluster-preserving null controls where assumptions permit; do not invent a universal 33% F1 or use an arbitrary favorable cutoff after inspection.

A deterministic label-leak defect blocks final admission. Other baseline associations require a recorded repair or an explicitly limited claim such as 'resistant to the listed rules on this diagnostic population.' Failure of a finite baseline suite never establishes general shortcut immunity. Freeze any repair using development evidence, then evaluate fresh untouched groups.

### 7. Add certified paired and representation controls

Use several types of related comparisons, not one perturbation recipe:

- A small interior coefficient, operator, sign or interval change with a newly verified different label, while preserving comparable outer structure.
- Label-preserving representation changes with proved semantic/domain equivalence.
- Exact affine input/output transformations with their correct label relation: positive output scaling and affine additions preserve curvature; negative output scaling swaps convex/concave and preserves neither; nonzero affine input changes require the corresponding transformed interval.
- Cases where interval awareness matters and cases where it does not, so domain-change heuristics cannot become a new shortcut.

Every member is independently certified and carries the relation, common group and verified transformation preconditions. Not every random edit should flip a label. Pair success requires both certified labels correct; a prediction change alone is not success. Representation agreement and mathematical correctness are separate measurements, since two agreeing answers may both be wrong.

Freeze pair/control allocations inside the consuming study's existing task count or define a separate explicit diagnostic panel. Count every member and request; do not append unpaid, unreported control calls. A transformation variant is not an additional independent mathematical family.

For failure reduction, preserve domain validity, relevant mechanism, reference proof and the declared failure predicate. Certify each reduction step and keep the original/reduction trace. Offline reductions can minimize a verifier/software defect; demonstrating preserved model failure requires separately accounted inference. Never substitute a reduced case into the original frozen score.

### 8. Separate development, validation and fresh evaluation

Development permits generator changes and exposed calibration outcomes, with all successes/failures/revisions retained. Validation checks the frozen baseline and split policy; changes informed by its labels or diagnostics make it development data for the next version. Final evaluation is generated only after grammar, thresholds, parameters, quotas and analysis rules are frozen.

Use different seeds and disjoint significant core/transformation groups across partitions. Hold out selected composition motifs or mechanism combinations in a separately named structural-generalization panel; it needs its own feasible coverage and cannot silently alter the matched family mix of Module 15's main depth study. Fresh parameter values from an exposed template are parameter holdout, not family holdout.

Record which models/settings saw development tasks or influenced changes. Where practical, reserve final model identities separately from calibration identities, but do not claim that aliases are independent architectures or that unseen requested IDs establish independence. Module 15's required Astra cell remains required. Public exposure and seed changes do not prove absence of training contamination.

Keep evaluation seeds/candidates out of public previews until the declared exposure/release point. Freeze hashes or another reviewable commitment before inference; release the exact artifact and reproduction information afterward under the project's publication policy. Seeds are experiment metadata, not provider credentials; provider keys never enter generation artifacts.

Once final outcomes are inspected, the population is exposed. Any repair, case replacement, baseline tuning or favorable rerun belongs to a new exploratory version and, for new confirmation claims, fresh held-out groups. Retain the previous result and its exposure history.

### 9. Keep ordinary sampling and selected challenge results separate

Maintain an ordinary certified sample drawn under the frozen grammar, quotas and reference rules without conditioning admission on model mistakes. Separately identify any panel selected by shortcut difficulty, adaptive search or development-model failures, with its selection rule and exposure ledger.

Do not call either panel representative of all mathematical functions. A shortcut filter changes the population and can create an inverse shortcut. Selecting only cases where one rule fails can make negating that rule successful. Publish heuristic-supporting and contradicting controls and describe the selected distribution explicitly.

If only the selected panel is executed, report only its conditional score. Do not impute scores for the ordinary sample or compare their percentages as if task populations were matched. Module 15 retains a single declared main population unless its owner records a pre-run amendment; this module does not double that study's workload.

### 10. Preserve scoring, uncertainty and execution boundaries

Use the existing label-extraction and headline metrics policy with the consuming study's explicit trial identity. Report full planned/dispatched/included/valid-correct/valid-wrong/invalid/refused/truncated/infrastructure/unknown/unattempted populations. Conditional accuracy on valid labels may be supplemental only; it cannot hide unusable replies from headline accuracy.

Report task, significant-core group, broad-family, sequence-pattern and repeated-request counts separately. Describe any sampling uncertainty as conditional on the declared synthetic distribution and group/exchangeability assumptions. Preserve complete pairs and all related depths during resampling. A fixed-suite observed score and an estimate of performance on future draws are different claims. Headline scores remain task-weighted under the existing policy; any group-weighted summary is separately named, with unequal group sizes and the chosen estimand explicit.

D-12's current bootstrap and stronger failure gate remain unchanged, including its minimum 20 effective groups overall and three per broad family for its eligible intervals. The implemented bootstrap expects three observations per task and at most 256 cases, so it cannot directly analyze Module 15's single-trial 800-case population. A new bounded research analysis must be separately versioned, justified and tested before use; otherwise report intervals unavailable. A single valid wrong answer can support a point counterexample, not D-12's repeated-error finding.

This module's planning/offline work adds zero model calls. Any later model-assisted development checks belong to Module 15's existing shared 30-send development allowance when supplying that study. Final requests remain 800 per frozen model/settings cell. Paired panels, reductions or roster additions do not create free calls; a distinct study needs its own pre-run numeric accounting. Use the official isolated OpenAI SDK, observable zero automatic retries and bounded shared concurrency under existing development instructions when live execution is later authorized by the study.

## Validation

Test normal cases and adversarial boundaries, including:

- A truly convex expression outside sufficient DCP recognition; a failed rule must remain inconclusive unless another proof succeeds.
- Affine/constant overlap; invalid log/square-root domains; zero denominators; overflow, precision/work exhaustion and unsupported atoms.
- Interior curvature changes missed by all fixed coarse probes, alongside cases correctly classified by those same probes.
- Domain-sensitive simplifications, inverse operations, affine/negation wrappers, reassociated sums and repeated-atom closed forms.
- Different syntax with known equivalence; different domains on one core; fresh seeds reusing an exposed significant core.
- A label-flipping pair, a label-preserving pair, two agreeing wrong predictions and a prediction flip with one wrong member.
- Serial/parallel replay, cancelled batches, exhausted proposal cells, partial checkpoints, altered versions/certificates and nondeterministic membership.
- Candidate count reconciliation, route/label imbalance and timeouts preserved without model-driven replacement.
- Root/sign/coefficient baselines, an inverse-shortcut adversary, and sound proof-rule abstentions treated as unsolved.
- Perfect, majority-only, wrong-valid, malformed, refused, truncated, infrastructure, unknown and unattempted metric examples.
- Single-trial versus three-trial analysis, small-group interval unavailability and cross-depth/pair group preservation.
- An all-success model result that remains reported without changing membership to manufacture failures.

Implementation verification must include the affected tests and exact repository commands such as `bun run lint`, `bun run typecheck` and `bun run build`; run `bun run numerics:test` and `bun run numerics:lint` when numerical code changes. ## Risks and Open Questions

- A broad random grammar may be mostly invalid or uncertifiable; reporting the accepted subset is necessary but does not remove selection bias.
- Deep independent proofs may only support restrictive families. Does their restriction permit a simple valid mathematical solution? That is a measured limitation, not a reason to falsify difficulty.
- More roots, branches or large coefficients can increase arithmetic/token burden without increasing the intended reasoning demand.
- Known normalization/group rules may miss semantic overlap or group too aggressively. Every supported split claim must state that boundary.
- Adaptive difficulty search can overfit development models, and selecting heuristic failures can create an inverse shortcut.
- Final seed secrecy and public task release do not establish contamination-free evaluation; provenance/exposure remain necessary.
- A finite baseline suite cannot prove absence of all shortcuts. Hardness must remain model-, budget- and population-specific empirical evidence.
- All target counts, especially Module 15's deeper strata, remain pending their independent feasibility and consumer-specific admission gates.
